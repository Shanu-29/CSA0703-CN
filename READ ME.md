When I sent a ping between two PCs through the switch, Packet Tracer's simulation showed the frame going only to the destination port — the switch had learned each PC's MAC address and forwarded traffic directly to it. When I replaced the switch with a hub and repeated the test, the same frame was broadcast out to every connected port, even though only one PC was the actual recipient. This showed me how hubs create unnecessary traffic and a higher chance of collisions, since every device shares the same channel and has to wait if two try to send at once. Switches avoid this by using MAC address tables to send data only where it's needed, which is why switches replaced hubs in almost all modern networks.
